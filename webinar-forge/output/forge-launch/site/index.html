Record one short clean sample. The model clones it on your own hardware and reads anything you type from then on. You are never on camera, and there is no second take because there was never a first.

Point the importer at hand-built webinar HTML plus a narration file. It carries narration across verbatim, maps what it recognises, and tells you exactly which slides it could not classify.

Straight Answers

Including the parts that are not good news

Do I need a graphics card?
No. The engine auto-detects CUDA or Apple silicon and falls back to your CPU. CPU works — it is just slower, so start with a short deck before committing to a 40-slide one.
Does my voice sample leave my machine?
No. The model runs locally and nothing is uploaded. Samples are also excluded when you repackage the zip, because a sample is all anyone needs to generate unlimited speech in that person's voice.
Does it process payments?
No. Pricing buttons are plain links. You point them at your own Stripe, PayPal or checkout URL.
Does it send email?
No. Opt-ins from the gate append to a JSONL file on your server. Export them into whatever you already send with.
Does it handle HTTPS?
No. The built-in server does not terminate TLS. For a public domain put nginx or Caddy in front of it, or copy the site folder to any static host.
Is the voice engine secured?
It has no authentication. Anyone who can reach its port can synthesize any installed voice, so it binds to loopback by default. Keep it there unless you put an authenticating proxy in front of it.
Can I use it for more than one webinar?
Yes. Copy the config and change the words. There is no per-render fee, no cap and no seat count.
What if I already built webinars by hand?
The importer converts them. Narration carries across verbatim; headings, bullets, stats and myth beats are mapped where recognised, and anything it cannot classify is listed for you to review.
How long does a build take?
Voice synthesis dominates. For a 40-slide deck: roughly 5–15 minutes on a CUDA GPU, 15–40 on Apple silicon, hours on CPU alone. Video render is 3–15 minutes depending on hardware.
